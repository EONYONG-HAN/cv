Research

I develop computational methods for transcriptome reconstruction, multi-omics integration, and immune-response interpretation. My dissertation connects computational performance with biological interpretation by examining how data selection and partitioning shape the evidence available for inference.

My work includes semantic read partitioning for parallel de novo assembly (Deep-SemP), transcriptome reconstruction in a non-model plant, benchmarking of multi-omics study designs, and phase-specific analysis of immune response. Across these studies, I examine what performance metrics capture, what biological information is retained, and where interpretation remains limited by the data and analysis design.

My background combines computer science with experimental protein research. Before focusing on bioinformatics, I worked on recombinant protein expression, purification, and crystallization. This experience informs my interest in connecting machine-learning representations with biological questions.
